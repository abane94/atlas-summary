Comprehensive Summary
=====================

Gerk's role in the Mother's Prayer is confirmed by the Navi
-----------------------------------------------------------

*   The party recites the **Mother's Prayer/Prophecy**, the same verse previously heard echoing through the stronghold.
    
*   Sylan explains his conclusion that **Gerk is the "child" referenced in the prophecy** and the one destined to open the doors.
    
*   Plymouth Gawater privately questions Gerk to determine whether Gerk truly accepts this role rather than simply being identified by prophecy.
    
*   Gerk repeatedly states that he is simply "doing his thing": traveling with friends, fighting enemies, and helping where he can. He does not see himself as a chosen hero.
    
*   Plymouth emphasizes that accepting the prophecy is **a choice**, not an unavoidable destiny.
    

New lore about the Mother's Prayer
----------------------------------

Plymouth provides significant historical context about the prophecy:

*   The Mother's Prayer is an ancient Navi prophecy/prayer whose original author or seer is unknown.
    
*   The prophecy has been recorded many times throughout history by many different authors.
    
*   Rather than being a single written document, Plymouth compares it to:
    
    *   the **tree** = the Mother's Prayer itself
        
    *   the **fruit** = each chosen "son or daughter" produced by the prophecy.
        
*   Previous "children" of the prophecy have existed throughout history.
    
*   Plymouth has personally met **three previous sons/daughters of the tree**:
    
    *   one accepted the role
        
    *   two rejected or abandoned it ("fell from the tree")
        
*   The prophecy is said to rewrite itself over time depending on the choices made by its chosen individual.
    
*   Most of these chosen individuals historically came from **Midgard**.
    

Gerk is warned about difficult choices ahead
--------------------------------------------

Plymouth explains that:

*   The prophecy traditionally follows a pattern:
    
    *   catastrophic events
        
    *   emergence of a hero
        
    *   increasingly difficult moral choices
        
*   Gerk has already faced difficult decisions, but the hardest choices are still approaching.
    
*   Plymouth believes the "growing season" is nearing its end, implying the prophecy is approaching its culmination.
    

The Navi seek Gerk's consent
----------------------------

Rather than forcing Gerk into the role, Plymouth repeatedly asks whether he wishes to continue.

Important points:

*   Gerk questions how anyone can truly know the prophecy refers to him.
    
*   Plymouth argues that the prophecy already accurately referenced known events and people connected to Gerk.
    
*   Sylan admits he intentionally kept information about the prophecy from Gerk until recently because he was uncertain and knew the Navi had plans involving him.
    
*   Plymouth reassures the party that the Navi are not trying to manipulate or exploit Gerk, but believe helping the chosen individual benefits Atlas as a whole.
    

Gerk accepts
------------

After considerable discussion:

*   Plymouth asks whether Gerk wishes to be "plucked from the tree" instead of "falling from it."
    
*   Gerk agrees, saying that if accepting the role helps Atlas, then he is willing.
    
*   Plymouth enthusiastically accepts his decision, noting that it has been approximately **1,500 years** since someone accepted the role.
    

The party is drawn into the prophecy
------------------------------------

Following Gerk's acceptance:

*   Plymouth addresses the rest of the party.
    
*   The companions are now tied to Gerk's role in the Mother's Prayer.
    
*   Their participation is also voluntary.
    
*   Gerk remains the central figure whose choices carry the greatest weight.
    
*   Plymouth cautions Gerk not to let that importance go to his head, emphasizing the seriousness of what lies ahead.

Characters / Concepts
=====================

Mother's Prayer / Mother's Prophecy
-----------------------------------

### New Information

*   Ancient Navi prophecy with an unknown origin.
    
*   Preserved through many different written accounts.
    
*   Functions as both prophecy and sacred prayer.
    
*   Historically used by the Navi to identify individuals destined to aid Atlas.
    
*   Can rewrite itself based on the choices of its chosen individual.
    
*   Follows recurring story patterns involving catastrophe, a hero, and difficult choices.
    

* * *

Sons and Daughters of the Tree
------------------------------

### New Information

*   Title given to individuals chosen by the Mother's Prayer.
    
*   Plymouth compares the prophecy to a tree that periodically bears fruit.
    
*   Multiple chosen individuals have existed throughout history.
    
*   Plymouth personally knew three previous chosen individuals:
    
    *   one accepted the calling,
        
    *   two rejected it.
        
*   Gerk is believed to be the newest "fruit" of the tree.
    

* * *

Navi
----

### New Information

*   The Mother's Prayer is considered sacred among the Navi.
    
*   The Navi historically used it to identify people who would assist in exploring or protecting Atlas.
    
*   Most previous chosen individuals came from Midgard.
    
*   According to Plymouth, the Navi's interest in Gerk is intended to benefit Atlas rather than exploit him.
    

* * *

Gerk

*   Officially becomes the newest accepted "son of the tree," according to Plymouth.

*   **Navi** → spelling uncertain (_Navi_, _Na'vi_, _Návi_, etc.).
