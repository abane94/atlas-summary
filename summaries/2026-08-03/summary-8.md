Comprehensive Summary — Keystone Selection and the New Keepers of Atlas
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

*   The party completed the process of choosing which realms they would protect as **keepers of the keystones**. Plymouth presented each remaining party member with a choice of realm to oversee.
    
*   Walker reflected on his past connection to **Lorel** and his home there, but ultimately decided that he had changed since those earlier adventures. With Glimmund’s encouragement, he chose **the Reach** instead, feeling that it was a better fit for who he had become.
    
    *   Glimmund reminded Walker that home is not only where someone comes from, but where they choose to belong.
        
    *   Walker acknowledged that his time in Lorel was important but no longer defined him.
        
    *   Plymouth gave Walker the **Reach keystone**, describing it as the largest of the arches with much unexplored territory, but one that Walker and Glimmund could traverse easily.
        
*   Dana was offered the choice of protecting **Patel**, a realm she had previously expressed interest in reaching because of its connection to time and an extravagant business.
    
    *   Dana accepted the responsibility and pressed the keystone into her chest while bowing.
        
    *   She declared she would be honored to guard **the center of the rings of Atlas**.
        
    *   Plymouth bowed in response, acknowledging her acceptance.
        
*   Ord was offered a choice between the remaining realms:
    
    *   **Feldspar** — a realm of magic, science, and alchemy.
        
    *   **Lith** — a realm that has rejected magic but contains misunderstood supernatural phenomena.
        
*   Ord chose **Feldspar** because it aligned with his interest in combining magic and science.
    
    *   Plymouth explained that Feldspar was a place of experimentation, magic, and alchemical discovery.
        
    *   Ord accepted the Feldspar keystone and vowed to watch over it.
        
*   Plymouth clarified that Lith should not be underestimated:
    
    *   Although the people of Lith have sworn off magic, they are not ignorant.
        
    *   Their understanding of supernatural forces manifests as folklore, fairy tales, cryptids, and stories.
        
    *   A person like Ord or Ciri might even be viewed as a deity because of their abilities.
        
*   Ciri/Siri returned and was offered the final available choices:
    
    *   Lorel
        
    *   Toroff
        
    *   Lith
        
*   Ciri chose **Lith**.
    
    *   Plymouth warned that Lith’s people might call her a witch if she openly uses magic, but she would likely become part of their history and legends.
        
    *   Their lack of understanding of their own magic means supernatural events become myths and stories.
        
    *   Ciri received the Lith keystone.
        
*   Plymouth then reflected on the situation:
    
    *   There were originally **eight keystones and eight intended keepers**, but the party now only had six members.
        
    *   Two keystones remain unclaimed:
        
        *   **Toroff**
            
        *   **Lorel**
            
    *   Plymouth and Kit Anger will temporarily act as stewards of these two realms until others can take up the mantle.
        
*   Plymouth noted the irony of the party’s current situation:
    
    *   The party originally came seeking information about the lich threatening the keystones.
        
    *   Instead of merely finding ways to stop him, they have become the protectors of the very objects he seeks.
        
    *   The responsibility has shifted from hunting the threat to preventing the keystones from falling into enemy hands.
        
*   Plymouth reminded the party:
    
    *   They are now the **keepers of the keystones**.
        
    *   The stones are powerful and require care.
        
    *   He cannot dictate how they use them, but he can guide them.
        
*   The session ended with Plymouth turning to Gerk and asking if he understood what accepting this mantle meant, hinting that becoming a keeper made him something greater than he was before.
